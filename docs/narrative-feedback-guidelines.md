# Faculty-facing narrative feedback

After completing the course-design review, append a faculty-facing narrative feedback draft to the review report. Use the finalized findings as its source; the narrative supplements the criterion tables and does not replace them. It is a draft for institutional review, not authorization to email faculty or post feedback in Canvas.

## Draft requirements

1. Include the exact reviewed course name in a subject line, such as `Subject: Course review feedback for [Course name]`.
2. Open with specific, evidenced strengths and a supportive tone. Do not invent strengths when evidence is limited; acknowledge observed work and explain the review's scope candidly.
3. Address every item rated **Moderate Revision** or **Major Revision** in the finalized review. Preserve the substance and priority of each finding, even when several are combined in one paragraph.
4. Group related concerns into themes and write connected prose rather than reproducing the rubric as a checklist.
5. Address observed accessibility barriers and institutional compliance concerns before lower-impact refinements. Preserve evidence limitations: an unresolved check is a verification need, not a confirmed violation or certification of compliance.
6. Give each improvement area a concrete next step, tied to the affected course content or activity. Use the review's action plans and verified locations; avoid vague advice such as “improve accessibility.”
7. Include at least three distinct, practical teaching tips for the next term, tailored to the course where evidence permits. Frame these as suggestions rather than additional deficiencies. Examples include a weekly announcement connecting objectives to tasks, a brief formative check with explanatory feedback, and a scheduled instructor discussion followed by a summary of common misconceptions.
8. Close by reinforcing continuous improvement and offering institutional support, such as consultation with the Center for Teaching and Learning or appropriate accessibility/technology staff. Do not invent named contacts, links, services, or commitments.
9. Use a faculty-facing, professional, non-punitive tone. Discuss course design and learner needs without attributing motives or judging instructor performance beyond the review's evidence.
10. Keep the complete draft within **600 words**, including the subject line, greeting, body, and closing. Compress related findings without omitting required concerns.

## Final verification

Before delivering the report, build an internal coverage map from each Moderate/Major criterion ID to the narrative passage that addresses it and its concrete next step. Compare against the finalized criterion records, not an earlier draft. Confirm all required IDs are covered, accessibility/compliance priorities come first, at least three next-term tips are identifiable, and the complete draft contains no more than 600 whitespace-separated words. Recheck that strengths and concerns agree with the evidence and that suggestions do not become unsupported findings.

Retain the coverage map and word count with the run's validation artifacts when a run directory exists; keep this reviewer checklist out of the faculty-facing prose. If the review is partial, label the narrative as based on a partial review and carry forward material uncertainty. If no Moderate/Major items exist, say so only when the completed review supports that conclusion, then focus on strengths and next-term suggestions.
