# CUOCDR review guidelines adapted from OSCQR

Prepared October 13, 2026 from the CUOCDR v0.9 rubric and the public [SUNY OSCQR standards and explanations](https://oscqr.suny.edu/). All 50 numbered OSCQR standard pages were retrieved and inspected for relevant guidance. This is an adapted reviewer reference, not a replacement rubric or a claim of SUNY/Cedarville approval.

## Authority and use

Internal reviewer reference only. Apply [internal review checks](internal-review-checks.md) for assessment consistency and the user-designated CTL Accessibility Checklist, including bounded verification. Master/blueprint treatment below is our internal review policy, not an official university exception.

**CUOCDR controls the review.** Preserve its 42 criterion IDs, seven categories, rating labels, and documented applicability. The source rubric numbers criteria 1–24 and 26–43; do not add criterion 25. OSCQR supplies explanations, observable evidence, and improvement ideas where the meaning fits. The mappings below are our interpretation of fit, not an official crosswalk.

OSCQR describes its process as formative course-design improvement. Use that practical orientation: identify evidence, explain an observed gap, and suggest a proportionate improvement. Do not turn every example on an OSCQR page into a prerequisite for Present. [OSCQR purpose](https://oscqr.suny.edu/)

Mapping labels:

- **Close:** the main criterion has a close counterpart; retain any CU-specific wording.
- **Combined:** several OSCQR standards inform one CU criterion.
- **Partial:** OSCQR explains only part of the CU criterion.
- **CU-only:** no OSCQR counterpart establishes the requirement.

For browser review, record the course ID/name, view used, inspection date, scope, page URL, and relevant evidence. Walk the course/module sequence and maintain an inventory so unvisited content does not silently count as reviewed. A screenshot supports visual findings; it does not prove hidden semantics or access for every learner. Inspect linked documents and media when they are needed to judge a criterion. Record blocked resources and sampling explicitly; a sampled result is not a course-wide verification.

Separate these evidence states from CU ratings:

- **Verified:** the inspected evidence supports the finding, within the stated scope.
- **Not evidenced:** the required element was not found after inspecting the relevant locations; describe the search scope.
- **Not verified:** access, browser capabilities, sampling, or missing institutional guidance prevents a conclusion.
- **Not applicable:** the course does not use the relevant content/tool and the criterion legitimately does not apply; give a short reason.

A verified deficiency can support a revision rating. An unverified technical feature should produce a verification step, not an invented pass/fail. Retain the CU ratings and time guidance; OSCQR examples do not establish CU severity. Treat assigned blueprint placeholders according to the internal reviewer policy: rate intentional master/blueprint scaffolds Present with instructor completion reminders, without claiming personalization is already complete. Review planned design in pre-launch courses; do not require actual instructor messages, grades, or learner participation to have occurred.

After completing the review, place a faculty-facing narrative feedback draft near the top of the report, after course identity/status and a brief limitations note and before the rubric tables, using [the narrative feedback guidelines](narrative-feedback-guidelines.md). Follow that guide for coverage, priorities, unscored next-term teaching tips and the word limit. Keep its claims consistent with the finalized evidence and limitations.

## 3. Design, Layout, and Accessibility

| CU ID and focus | Fit and source | Adapted guideline and browser evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **9. Logical, consistent, uncluttered layout/navigation** | Close: [O16 organization](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard16/) | Compare modules and page types for predictable organization, clear titles, consistent cues, and a logical learning sequence. Follow the learner path to readings, activities, and assessments. Identify concrete confusion such as contradictory labels or deeply buried required work; do not demand identical styling for every resource. |
| **10. Manageable sections and white space** | Close: [O17 chunking](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard17/) | Inspect dense pages for readable sections, useful breaks, and spacing. Assess the actual reading experience, including scrolling and narrower layouts where available. Recommend splitting content by meaningful topics rather than imposing an arbitrary word count or number of pages. |
| **11. Readable text/background contrast** | Close: [O18 contrast](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard18/) | Inspect course-authored text over backgrounds, including banners, images, slides, and documents. Record hard-to-read examples with their locations. Visual inspection can reveal problems but cannot establish a measured contrast threshold; use supported measurement when needed or mark that portion unverified. |
| **12. Clear, well-written instructions** | Close: [O19 instructions](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard19/) | Check that the learner can determine what to do, where/how to do it, when it is due, and how the task relates to learning and assessment. Compare overview, assignment, discussion, and quiz instructions for consistency. Report ambiguity that affects execution, not merely preferred wording. |
| **13. Grammar and spelling** | Close: [O20 mechanics](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard20/) | Perform a separate mechanical review of inspected course text. Record the exact location, issue, and proposed correction. Preserve discipline-specific terminology, quotations, and intended voice. Verify suspected spacing/formatting defects in the rendered original; do not mark extraction artifacts as source errors. |
| **14. Titles, headings, and readable document structure** | Close: [O21 hierarchy](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard21/) | Check meaningful titles, scannable sections, and a consistent hierarchy. Where browser-supported structure inspection is available, distinguish actual headings from text that is merely bold or large. A visually clear page does not by itself verify semantic heading structure. |
| **15. No flashing/blinking or red/green text** | Partial: [O22 flashing/blinking](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard22/), related [O36 color independence](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard36/) | Inspect motion/animation and text colors. OSCQR directly supports avoiding flashing/blinking; CU adds the red/green text restriction, which remains independently applicable. Do not narrow the CU rule to red/green combinations or color-only meaning. Document observed examples; a static screenshot does not establish animation behavior. |
| **16. Sans-serif, at least 12 pt** | Close: [O23 typography](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard23/) | Assess only Canvas-native page and activity elements under the user-specified CU 16 scope. Exclude attached and external documents, including PDF, Word and slides. Use supported style/font inspection; screenshot appearance alone does not establish exact point size. Preserve CU wording and disclose measurement scope. |
| **17. CU-accessible tables** | Combined: [O24 linear alternatives](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard24/), [O25 context/title](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard25/), [O26 headers](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard26/) | Prefer paragraphs/lists for non-tabular information. For real data tables, inspect a meaningful title/context, appropriate row/column headings, clear relationships, and a sensible reading sequence. Visible bold labels are not proof of programmatic headers. Apply the designated CTL checklist’s row/column header rule and assess it directly. The preserved rubric’s missing link does not block that check; disclose any additional requirement that remains unavailable. Do not require a separate caption solely on the basis of this checklist. A table's existence is not itself a defect. |
| **18. Slide layouts and unique titles** | Close: [O27 slides](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard27/) | Open slide resources and inspect individual slides for consistent predefined layouts and meaningful unique titles, including continued sections. Dense text or unclear purpose can be an improvement observation where it affects this criterion. If only a recording/flattened PDF is accessible, record that underlying slide layouts were not verified. |
| **19. Simple, non-automatic slide transitions** | Close: [O28 transitions](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard28/) | Inspect a usable presentation/playback where available. Check whether learners control progression and whether transitions are simple. A static PDF does not verify the original slide timing/transition configuration; classify that check as unverified if the relevant presentation is inaccessible. |
| **20. Accessible text, preferably HTML** | Close: [O34 text formats](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard34/) | Prefer directly readable course HTML. Open documents and check whether text is available as text rather than only scanned images. Inspect accessible structure/reading order where supported. Selectable text or a successfully opened PDF is insufficient to prove full assistive-technology readability. Image-only content needs an accessible text alternative. |
| **21. Non-text equivalents and video-only descriptions** | Close: [O35 equivalents](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard35/) | Exclude scanned files under the user-specified CU 21 exception. Inspect other meaningful images/diagrams for appropriate alternative descriptions, complex visuals for sufficient explanation, and audio/video for the relevant captions/transcripts/descriptions. Distinguish decorative from informative visuals. Confirm caption availability and inspect accuracy within the stated scope. CU's expectation of Kaltura captions is a local expectation, not observed proof. A CC button, filename, or provider brand alone does not establish quality. |
| **22. Meaning understandable without color** | Close: [O36 color independence](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard36/) | Inspect charts, highlights, status indicators, instructions, and legends. Check that labels, shapes, text, or other cues preserve meaning without the color distinctions. Contrast and color independence are separate checks; a readable red/green chart can still rely on color alone. |
| **23. Descriptive hyperlink text** | Close: [O37 links](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard37/) | Read link labels outside their surrounding sentences: can the learner understand destination/action? Flag ambiguous repeated labels such as “click here” or “read more” when they fail the criterion. Recommend meaningful destination/topic labels. Opening the link checks its destination; successful navigation alone does not establish descriptive text. |

## 7. Christian Perspective: retain CU guidance separately

OSCQR does not supply a Christian-perspective standard. The following checks are derived from the local CU rubric, not attributed to SUNY. Do not use generic diversity, critical-thinking, or collaboration guidance as proof that these criteria are met.

| CU ID | CU-derived reviewer check |
| --- | --- |
| **40. Christian approach to content** | Identify substantive places where course content is approached from a Christian perspective. Record the relationship to the discipline/topic; distinguish content-level integration from an unrelated decorative reference. |
| **41. Appropriate Scripture references** | Identify Scripture references and inspect their relevance to the surrounding material. Do not treat mere quotation counts as proof of appropriateness or add a minimum frequency. Flag interpretive uncertainty for the instructor rather than inventing a CU theological standard. |
| **42. Engagement with challenges from outside the faith, as appropriate** | Inspect where relevant alternative claims/challenges are surfaced and engaged. Judge against the subject and the criterion's “as appropriate” qualification. Explain a legitimate N/A; do not require a controversy in every module. |
| **43. Learner Christian-perspective response** | Look for activities/questions that ask learners to consider course content from a Christian perspective and respond. Instructor statements alone are different evidence from a learner response opportunity. Do not require a particular response format absent CU instructions. |

## Boundaries: what was deliberately not imported

- **OSCQR 4, printable syllabus:** useful as an optional access improvement under CU 1/3/20, but not a new requirement to provide a separate PDF. [O4](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard4/)
- **OSCQR 7, delivery-mode label:** useful when it clarifies course expectations under CU 3, but not an additional scored CU criterion. Do not adopt its generalization that fully online courses must lack synchronous requirements. [O7](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard7/)
- **OSCQR 31, real-world applications:** used as an example for CU 26/35, not a separate mandatory case-study/project requirement. [O31](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard31/)
- **OSCQR 32, OER/free/low-cost materials:** no direct CU criterion. Do not lower CU ratings because commercial materials are used. Licensing evidence still applies under CU 27. [O32](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard32/)
- **OSCQR 49, populated/current gradebook:** no standalone CU criterion and poor fit for requiring delivery evidence in a pre-launch review. Do not inspect student grades or require posted grades. Learner progress/feedback design can inform CU 34/37 without importing O49 as a separate requirement. [O49](https://oscqr.suny.edu/standard49/)
- **SUNY-specific policy, offices, legal statements, or products:** replace campus examples only with actual CU resources when available. Examples such as a particular LMS, plagiarism product, badge system, introductory quiz, or welcome video are options, not CU obligations.
- **OSCQR page suggestions beyond the matched criterion:** use only when a recommendation addresses observed CU evidence. Do not import arbitrary response deadlines, posting counts, media quotas, or a syllabus-quiz requirement.

## Instructions ready for the future browser-review skill

1. Load the CU rubric and this reference. Use original CU IDs and labels; consult the linked OSCQR explanation when deeper interpretation is needed.
2. Establish instructor/pre-launch or student-experience scope. Inspect drafts for pre-launch review; record the view and publication/access limitations.
3. Inventory and navigate the course, recording pages/resources inspected and remaining coverage. Read substantive content and activity requirements; do not score by checking titles alone.
4. Apply the matched guidance to each criterion. Preserve CU-specific extensions and blueprint exceptions. Use source-linked evidence, observed deficiencies, concise improvements, and unresolved verification steps.
5. Keep the mechanical grammar findings separate and tie them to original text.
6. Check all 42 criteria are addressed, that evidence supports each rating, and that no OSCQR-only criterion or example became an unapproved requirement. Generate the review with explicit scope/limitations and actual artifact links.

Review remains read-only. Where the browser cannot expose a technical property, retrieve it through an available read-only browser inspection capability or state that it is unverified. An API or exported bundle may be an optional evidence supplement, but neither is required for the primary visual review. Do not silently switch to an API workflow when browser-only review was requested.
