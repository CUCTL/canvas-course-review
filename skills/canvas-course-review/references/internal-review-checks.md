# Internal review checks

These are operating instructions for this skill, not amendments to CUOCDR or official university guidance. Keep the official rubric's wording, IDs and ratings unchanged. Master/blueprint and optional-training treatment remains an internal interpretation under [review policy](review-policy.md) and [template guidance](template-placeholders.md). Do not present those interpretations as university-approved exceptions.

## 1. Compare assessment instructions across locations

For each major assessment or assessment type, compare the module overview, assignment/discussion prompt, attached rubric or scoring directions, configured quiz/assignment settings, and applicable instructor guide. Inspect every distinct major assessment; a repeated template can be checked as a group only after confirming that its actual requirements agree. Check smaller recurring tasks when their instructions/settings differ or a conflict is found.

Compare the following, using API content/settings and local documents first:

- Purpose and intended learning; assigned passage, topic, reading or required media.
- What students produce: written work, recording, file type, length and required sections.
- Individual/group work, required/optional participation, and alternative ways to complete a task.
- Criteria, required audience, points, weights and what the rubric actually grades.
- Time limits, permitted resources, attempts and result visibility where relevant.
- Due-date sequence, first post versus replies, and whether dates belong to a template or an actual offering.

Record the inspected locations and exact conflicts in the internal evidence register or working notes. Identify the correct requirement only when evidence establishes it; otherwise ask the course owner to decide. Do not choose a passage, create grading weights, or add a requirement simply to resolve a mismatch. A review does not authorize edits.

In the report, describe the conflict in plain language, group the repair into one coherent task, and link all affected locations. A problem can affect more than one criterion, but avoid presenting it as several unrelated repairs. After a repair is verified, update every affected finding and narrative passage so completed work is not still described as open.

## 2. Group related findings into a coordinated repair

Group findings when the evidence shows that they arise from the same conflicting requirement or need one coordinated correction. Do not merge unrelated tasks merely because they concern the same assignment or share a broad theme such as accessibility.

- Name the task in faculty-facing language, such as “Make the final manuscript instructions agree.” Explain the problem and intended result once in the overview, linking every affected criterion.
- Retain every affected criterion's rating, evidence, uncertainty and sources. Grouping does not lower severity, combine ratings or turn a separate unresolved question into a verified finding.
- In the detailed tables, give each criterion the part of the correction that belongs to it. Keep each action next to its actual course destination. Do not repeat the whole repair list under every criterion or replace useful directions with “see above.”
- Include every location that must change: overview, prompt, rubric, quiz/settings page, or instructor guide. Keep decisions for the course owner explicit when the intended requirement is not established.
- Keep separate actions when the work or evidence differs. For example, correcting a document's text and checking a video's captions can share an overview theme but remain distinct tasks.

### Example

Suppose the final manuscript prompt describes a recording, the rubric grades a written study, and the module overview names a different passage. If the evidence supports treating these as one conflict, present one overview priority:

> Make the final manuscript instructions agree. Confirm the intended passage and written submission, then align the module overview, assignment prompt, rubric and instructor guide. This affects objectives/alignment (CU 4), instructions (CU 12), and assessment criteria (CU 36).

In the table actions, keep the work specific:

| Criterion | Its part of the coordinated correction |
| --- | --- |
| CU 4 | Align the module overview's passage and description of the work with the approved assignment. Link the affected overview. |
| CU 12 | State the written submission, required sections and format in the assignment prompt and instructor guide. Link both locations. |
| CU 36 | Make the rubric assess the work students are actually instructed to submit. Link the assignment/rubric location. |

These are illustrative instructions, not a finding about a new course. In a real report, use the inspected evidence and actual destinations. If a criterion has an additional unrelated concern, retain it as a separate action.

This is a reporting practice only. It does not introduce repair identifiers, status tracking, automatic rating changes or a new report schema; fix tracking is deferred to [issue 1](https://github.com/CUCTL/canvas-course-review/issues/1).

## 3. Accessibility checks and a stopping point

### Source and authority

Use the user-designated [CTL Accessibility Checklist, ADA-checklist_2026.docx](https://cedarvilleuniversity394.sharepoint.com/:w:/s/CTL/IQCNgOVC_dnJRqRtF8je-zZQAWRr-4rHuN9aZ-G8nEDmS0Y) for the accessibility rules below. Source metadata last modified August 5, 2026; read for this guidance October 20, 2026. The document describes itself as a faculty-focused subset of WCAG 2.1 A/AA, not an exhaustive standard. This section summarizes its checks; it does not change the source document or certify compliance. Refresh the linked source when a review depends on a later revision; if unavailable, identify this saved summary's date rather than claiming a live policy check.

The private source copy belongs in ignored local evidence, not the public skill package. Retain this source link and attribution with the working summary. OSCQR remains explanatory and must not add mandatory accessibility checks beyond the applicable CUOCDR criterion and designated checklist without a stated basis.

### Checklist rules to apply

| Area | Check from the designated document |
| --- | --- |
| Titles, headings and labels | Page titles describe their purpose. Headings and labels describe their topic. Heading levels follow their function and appear in order. |
| Lists | Use actual numbered/bulleted list styles rather than typed markers. |
| Links and emphasis | Link text identifies the destination or purpose. Use bold/italic styles for emphasis, not color alone; avoid underlining text that is not a link. |
| Tables | Identify applicable row and column headers, including the correct span relationships. Visible bold text alone does not establish table headers. |
| Images and charts | Give meaningful non-text content an equivalent text alternative. Explain complex images in surrounding text. Charts need a short label/title and a longer description of their type, main data/trends and meaning; include the underlying data table when possible. Evaluate decorative images in context rather than inventing information they do not convey. |
| Text | Use text instead of pictures of text whenever possible. |
| Color | Information must remain understandable without color as the only visual cue. |
| Contrast | Apply the checklist's stated ratios: 4.5:1 for text/images and 3:1 for non-text. Record measured foreground/background values. If another source would allow a different threshold, identify that difference instead of silently substituting it for the designated checklist. |
| Prerecorded media | Captions convey equivalent spoken-audio information. Audio-only media has a transcript including meaningful non-speech context, not only the spoken words. |
| Directions and navigation | Identify items by name rather than appearance alone. Provide multiple ways to reach pages, except when a page is a step in a process. |

Apply the Canvas-elements-only scope in [review policy](review-policy.md) for CU 16: exclude attached/external documents from typography checks. Retain separately supported checks under other criteria, including the CU 21 scanned-file exception.

The checklist does not state a blanket ban on red/green or a universal font-family/12-point rule. CUOCDR 15 and 16 still contain their own wording: retain those rubric checks, but distinguish a CUOCDR style requirement from a barrier established under the accessibility checklist. Do not describe every use of red, green or serif type as a demonstrated accessibility failure. Preserve and explain a conflict rather than silently rewriting either source.

The checklist supplies a concrete table-header rule for CU 17. Assess that rule from markup/document structure; do not leave every table unverified solely because the preserved rubric still says [Need Link]. State the scope assessed and identify any additional institutional requirement that actually remains unavailable. Do not invent a requirement for a caption on every table from this checklist alone.

### Verification boundaries

1. State the rule, content in scope and evidence needed before checking it. Inspect API HTML/settings and original document structure first. Inspect all in-scope instructional files locally; do not substitute a screenshot or a sample file for that document coverage.
2. Use browser checks only for unanswered rendered or functional questions. For repeated page layouts or media/tool workflows, choose representative examples covering each distinct pattern. Record exactly which pages, media sections, caption languages and functions were checked. Do not claim an untested item was inspected because it shares a provider or template.
3. For video samples, check that captions can be activated and compare the sampled spoken content with the displayed captions. For audio-only samples, compare the transcript with the audio and meaningful sound context. A caption file, selectable document text or scanner pass is evidence to inspect, not proof of an equivalent experience.
4. Expand the check when a concrete defect, different format/workflow or contradictory evidence appears. Check the affected items or pattern until the finding's scope is supported. Do not require an arbitrary number of browser visits or a complete specialist audit merely to finish a course-design review.
5. Stop when the stated question is answered within its recorded scope, or a concrete access/technical limit prevents further supported inspection. One ordinary retry or another available supported route is reasonable for a transient failure; do not repeatedly reopen the same blocked resource. Record the limit and the next useful step.
6. Keep a remaining required question unverified with a concrete next step. Do not treat the absence of exhaustive screen-reader, caption-synchronization or frame-by-frame flashing tests as a proven defect, nor add those tests as universal prerequisites from this checklist. A specific concern under CUOCDR, observed media or the required learner workflow can justify further checks or specialist help. A sample does not certify all media or every learner pathway.

For reports, distinguish an observed problem, a sampled result, and a check still needed. Ask a specialist to investigate a named resource/function or uncertainty, rather than requesting that they certify the entire course. Honor level 1's no-browser boundary; levels 2 and 3 use the same bounded verification, and level 3's blind assessment reuses the saved evidence.
