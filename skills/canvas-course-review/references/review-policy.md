# Review policy

Version 1.4, October 13, 2026. CUOCDR v0.9 is the rating authority. The preserved rubric has 42 items numbered 1–24 and 26–43; do not add item 25. Preserve category order and criterion wording.

## Ratings and evidence

Ratings: Present, Minor Revision, Moderate Revision, Major Revision, N/A. A null rating represents pending verification, not a sixth rating. Every rated criterion needs concise evidence and resolvable source IDs. Revision items need a concrete action plan; Present needs observed support; N/A needs a reason. Unresolved criteria need a verification step and uncertainty.

Evidence states:
- verified: inspected evidence supports the conclusion within the stated scope.
- not_evidenced: the required element was not found after inspecting relevant locations; document the search scope. This can support a revision when the search establishes an actual absence.
- not_verified: access, sampling, missing policy, or technical limits prevent a conclusion; leave rating null.
- not_applicable: a legitimate N/A, with rationale.

Use a deferred flag for identified blueprint/pre-launch instructor/contact/introduction placeholders; leave the rating null and record when/who should complete them. This exception does not exempt other design requirements or excuse missing personalization once an instructor is assigned.

Source time bands are guidance: Minor <20 minutes, Moderate <40 minutes, Major 60+ minutes. They overlap and leave 40–59 minutes unclear. Do not invent a numeric classification rule; explain ambiguous choices for instructor judgment.

## Institutional boundaries

Item 1 links to authenticated CU syllabus requirements; item 17 contains an unresolved [Need Link]. Obtain authoritative guidance when available, otherwise expose that limitation. Do not replace CU policy with OSCQR examples or claim legal/accreditation/accessibility certification.

Kaltura/video.cedarville.edu captions are expected, not automatically verified. Inspect available tracks/transcripts and content; provider identity alone is insufficient. Screenshots alone cannot establish hidden semantics, keyboard operation, screen-reader usability, or universal learner access. Vendor accessibility/privacy statements establish disclosure, not complete compliance.

Recommend hiding navigation only when unused status is established or the instructor confirms it. Evaluate documented interaction and feedback design in every mode, including existing/published courses, without requiring messages, grades, or learner participation to have occurred. Keep Christian Perspective findings evidence-based and specific to the rubric.

## Presentation

Text rating labels are authoritative; do not rely on color/icons. Review tables contain ID, criterion, rating, evidence, and action/verification. Show scope, snapshot, rubric version, source references, coverage limitations, deferred fields, priority actions, and mechanical grammar separately. Write the narrative after findings are finalized, then display it immediately after the course identity/date/mode/status and brief limitations note, before the rubric tables. Put full scope/limitations and stage outcomes after the tables and grammar findings. Keep the detailed source register, mappings, file checklist and inventory in a separate evidence register linked from the main report; suggestions must not become new unsupported deficiencies.

Report-generation status is separate from course quality. completed means all stages required by the selected review level were performed, even if technical/policy verification remains pending and is explicitly disclosed. partial means scope or a report stage remains unfinished. failed means no usable review was produced. Never turn failure text or missing stages into a clean success.

## Independent confirmation

For level 3, before presenting a new review, obtain a separate agent’s blind assessment of the same saved raw evidence and controlling policy. Freeze both assessments before comparison; reconcile against evidence rather than averaging. Then audit the final report and narrative for consistency, documenting that comparison is no longer blind. Reuse existing browser evidence unless a concrete gap requires more inspection. Preserve assessment/audit artifacts and disclose incomplete or contaminated confirmation. See [the confirmation workflow](blind-confirmation.md).

## Review levels

Offer three levels when the user has not selected one: API content + narrative (1), API content + necessary browser verification + narrative (2; recommended), and API content + necessary browser verification + blind assessment + narrative (3). Wait for an explicit user selection before course collection or review; silence and a preselected option do not select a level. The rubric, design-only boundary, grammar pass, narrative checklist, and report format apply to all levels. Level 1 cannot establish browser-dependent behavior; leave those conclusions unverified, with explicit limits and next steps. Levels 1–2 do not require independent confirmation. All levels require a primary consistency check and validation. Level 3 requires blind assessment, reconciliation, and the final policy audit. See [review levels](review-levels.md). Do not infer a deficiency from a check intentionally outside the selected level, or silently downgrade unfinished level-3 confirmation.

## Design-only boundary

Review planned and authored course design, not course delivery. Interaction criteria concern documented expectations, planned substantive contact, communication guidance, and opportunities designed into the course. Feedback criteria concern stated policies, assessment criteria, self-review opportunities, and designed feedback mechanisms. Do not evaluate actual instructor responsiveness, feedback execution/quality, student engagement/achievement, attendance, or actual accommodation implementation. Do not turn empty activity histories, unavailable real-learner sessions, or absent posted grades into design deficiencies.

Configured dates, navigation/visibility, media playback, document accessibility, quiz instructions and preview controls may be checked as design evidence. Actual enrolled-learner testing and delivery outcomes are outside this skill; their absence is a scope boundary, not unfinished design review. Missing documented design intent can remain pending, with a request to clarify the plan. Keep instructor personalization as preparation/deferred content where policy permits. Practical teaching tips are optional future suggestions and never evidence that delivery failed.
